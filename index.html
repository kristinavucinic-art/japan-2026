How this city works
The hotels Five minutes apart on foot, both in Dojima, both ten minutes from Osaka Station.
Osaka to Kyoto JR Special Rapid from Osaka Station. 28 minutes, ¥560, no reservation needed, every fifteen minutes from 6am. The Shinkansen runs from Shin-Osaka instead, so it saves nothing from Dojima.
Inside Kyoto Taxis for the hills, LUUP e-bikes for the flat stretches, trains where they go. Never the buses in foliage season.
Inside Osaka The Midosuji subway line runs north to south through everything we need: Umeda, Shinsaibashi, Namba, Dobutsuen-mae. It is the only line the Osaka days need.
Sunset 16:50. Kyoto temples start closing their gates from 16:00, so the day ends earlier than it feels as though it should.
Chawan-zaka, a Higashiyama lane, at sunset.
The one thing
Two mornings carry the whole Kyoto plan: Tofuku-ji at gate-open on Day 3 and Arashiyama by ten on Day 4. The early alarms will be hard, but these are the quietest hours the temples have all day — and the afternoons can be as gentle as needed to make up for it.

No tipping Not in restaurants, not in taxis, not for porters. Money left on the table causes confusion rather than delight.
Cash Japan is far more card-friendly than its reputation, but small temples, shrine entries and older counters are cash only. Carry ¥10,000 each as a floor.
Ratings Japanese reviewers are harsh. On Tabelog, 3.5 is genuinely very good and anything above 3.8 is exceptional. Google-star instincts do not apply.
Gifts Small wrapped items from home for anyone who hosts or helps. Low value, nicely presented.
Getting around
Shinkansen Green Car Nozomi on both long legs, through SmartEX. The Tokyo–Osaka leg on 23 November is booked: Nozomi 71, 12:55 from Shinagawa, arriving Shin-Osaka 15:15. The Osaka–Tokyo leg on 27 November is still to book in advance, to secure window seats on the Fuji side, which sell out early. Reservations change free up to four minutes before departure, so a late flight on arrival day only means moving the Tokyo–Osaka booking to a later train.
Luggage Our cases are total 157cm, under the 160cm threshold, so no oversized-baggage reservation is needed. Anything above 160cm must be booked in advance or refused at the gate. The 23 November train is booked in the row with the larger baggage area anyway, so there is no question of space on a public holiday.
Suica Taps on and off everything: local trains, subways, buses, convenience stores, vending machines. In Apple Wallet it tops up from a card, no station machine needed. How to add it.
Taxis Use the GO app. Doors open automatically — do not touch them. Drivers rarely speak English, so show the destination in Japanese.
Kyoto Trains and bikes, never buses. Kyoto buses in foliage season are the slowest way across the city. LUUP e-bikes cover the flat stretches.
Daylight and season
Sunset Around 16:50 in Kansai in late November, a little earlier in Tokyo. The useful photography light ends around 16:00.
Crowds Foliage season is peak season. The first hour after the temple gates open is the quietest of the day; by late morning the famous sites are full.
Foliage Each day’s foliage line follows the Weathernews forecast for that day’s key spot (Tofuku-ji, Arashiyama, Osaka Castle Park and the Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue), checked daily; the Japan Meteorological Corporation forecast gives a second view of peak colour by city.
Weather Cold mornings, mild afternoons; 13°/5° is typical. Layers, and shoes that handle wet stone steps.

Money and manners
Tipping Not expected in local restaurants and cafes. Fine dining adds a 10% service charge automatically; nothing more is needed on top.
Cash Octopus covers most small purchases. Market stalls and some red taxis are cash only — a few hundred Hong Kong dollars each is enough.
Pace Faster, louder and more direct than Japan. What reads as brusque is simply efficient.
Getting around
Octopus Runs the MTR, the Star Ferry, buses, trams, convenience stores and market stalls. Also required for Happy Valley race tickets.
MTR Fast, clean and simple. Tsim Sha Tsui station is two minutes from the Regent.
Star Ferry The fastest and cheapest crossing from Tsim Sha Tsui to Central, and the best eight minutes of transport in the city.
Taxis Red for the city, green for the New Territories. Many take cash or Octopus only. Uber is simpler where language is a barrier.
Walking Hong Kong is dense and walkable in a way Tokyo is not. The Mid-Levels Escalator does the hills on Hong Kong Island.
